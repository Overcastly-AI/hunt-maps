Ridgeline — Front-end redesign, Direction B of 3

The map is the entire product.

Thesis. Almost all interface is a failure to make the map speak for itself. Chrome is drawn as if it were printed on the map sheet — a neatline collar, a title-block marginalia strip, a legend that unrolls flush from the edge — rather than floated on top of it in cards, and it recedes to near-nothing until touched. Colour is spent in exactly one place, on the thing that changes what the map means right now; everything else is ink density on bone, the way a printed quad sheet separates fact from interpretation by weight rather than by rainbow. The map is never dimmed, blurred, or occluded to make room for a control — every summoned surface displaces the frame's own edge, never the ground underneath it.

Designed primarily for the low-light condition this product is actually used in — 05:30, pre-dawn, minimal ambient light, a screen that should not bloom and ruin night vision. Bright snow-glare at midday is real and different, not a myth to hand-wave: it gets its own, explicit, opt-in treatment (heavier ink casing, shown in the annex) rather than one contrast setting asked to serve both. See "Designed for which light" below.

Layout — the concept

The chrome is drawn as if printed on the map sheet itself, not floated over it: a neatline collar, a marginalia title-block, a legend that unrolls flush from the bottom edge. Every summoned surface is anchored to an edge or corner and displaces its own edge, never the ground — so wind/date/thermal ride as the summoned sheet's own sticky header instead of being covered by it, which is the specific bug this direction was warned about.

What I kept from the existing system, and why. ConditionsBar (packages/design) is rated 9/10 in the product audit for exactly the right reason — always visible, one row, height independent of content, states its value or says "not set" rather than hiding. I kept that principle in full: wind, date/time and thermal are never buried behind a tap anywhere in this direction. I changed only the form — from a floating glass strip to the map's own top marginalia and corner compass — because a glass panel is exactly the kind of "panel floating over the map" this direction exists to delete. The content and the always-on guarantee are the same; the material is not.

The one real risk taken

Confidence is rendered as ink density and texture, not as a coloured badge. Measured is solid fill; Inferred is 72%-opacity fill; Doctrine is a diagonal hatch; Assumed is a dotted outline on nothing. No hue is spent on it at all — it is the most literal possible reading of "luminance-ordered, not colour-coded," applied to the single most trust-critical signal in the product (whether a hunter can believe the bedding score under their thumb). The obvious, safer choice was a four-colour chip. I rejected it because a coloured badge reads as decoration the eye learns to ignore, and this signal cannot afford to become wallpaper — texture forces a half-second of actual reading. The real cost: it is less instantly legible than colour on first exposure, so it ships with its own key (bottom of this document) exactly the way a printed map ships a legend for a symbol nobody was born knowing.

A 2px rule (the same weight cartography uses for an index contour) separates them from Bedding likelihood below, which does carry a confidence mark.

Annex — proof, not just assertion

A minimal direction that only shows its empty state hasn't shown its hard parts. These four strips are the hard parts: how confidence actually reads, what all three offline states look like, how the chrome survives glare as well as dawn, and the measured proof that hiding things did not shrink the touch targets.

Confidence key — shipped with the product, not just in docs/EVIDENCE.md

Fact layers (slope, aspect, landform, hillshade) carry no mark at all — badging a validated algorithm "Measured" devalues the badge that matters. Only judgement layers get one, and it is always graded at the weakest input, never rounded up.

Measured

Published, peer-reviewed algorithm, validated here against analytic surfaces with a closed-form answer.

Inferred

Fitted to related data. The shape of the model is trusted; specific constants are borrowed from an adjacent population or context.

Doctrine

Widely accepted in wildlife-management practice. Not independently measured inside this product.

Assumed

A number the model needed and we chose. No citation supports it yet — treat the layer as a hypothesis.

Offline coverage — all three states

Never a single silent "offline ready" boolean. The ring's fill amount and stroke style carry the state; the sentence underneath always names the actual check that was run.

Ink casing — dawn default vs. glare, opt-in

Ordinary casing (a soft dark halo behind bone ink) already reads across dark forest and bright field alike — this is the default and it is not a mode, it's just how every mark is drawn. Snow-glare at midday is a different, harder problem: a single tap on the neatline corner switches to a heavier doubled halo with a solid backing chip behind numerals, because a soft halo alone washes out under direct glare. This is a real, user-set toggle, not an ambient-light-sensor guess — those are unreliable across phones and a hunter can tell what the sky is doing better than a lux sensor can.

Build notes. Self-contained: three type families and both backgrounds are embedded as base64 (fonts fetched once from Google Fonts' open-source repository and inlined — not loaded at runtime, no CDN request occurs when this file is opened). Backdrop is apps/web/screenshots/02b-desktop-map-only.png, downsampled to JPEG for a reviewable file size; a shipped build would keep the source PNG/vector tiles. All interactive elements are real <button>s so keyboard focus (:focus-visible, field-blue ring) and prefers-reduced-motion can be checked directly in a browser, not just asserted in prose. No red/green pairing anywhere; every state that matters is legible in greyscale (open dev tools → emulate vision deficiency → achromatopsia to check).

What I would need on a real device before trusting this. Whether the invisible 56px hit circles around the compass/coverage stamps stay clear of MapLibre's own pan/zoom gesture recognizer rather than fighting it; whether 450ms long-press-to-readout survives a bumpy truck ride (accelerometer jitter reads as movement and cancels the hold); real luminance metering of the default casing against a phone at minimum brightness in an unlit tree stand, not a designer's guess at "reads fine"; and whether Big Shoulders Display's condensed numerals stay legible at 13–16px on a scratched, sub-$300 Android screen — condensed faces are the first thing to break down at small sizes on bad glass.
